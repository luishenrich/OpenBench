A metric bank for client-specific LLM benchmarks

Companies do not need another copy of MMLU. They need a small scorecard, chosen from a library, that measures the task they actually run. This note is the research decision behind that library, and a map of what is in it.

How many metrics are warranted

The bank has one row per measurement a client benchmark can actually run. Public exams (MMLU, BIG-bench, GSM8K, HELM scenarios, Arena Elo) are priors, not metrics. Where Ragas and DeepEval name the same construct, one row is kept and the other name is an alias. Metrics marked avoid_as_primary exist so a selector can refuse BLEU, ROUGE, BERTScore, fluency, or embedding cosine as the sole quality score. Operational metrics are measured on the same run as quality. Only utility_axis=quality scores enter the 0–1 quality average, and they must already be higher-better. Lower-better risk rates are gates. A client scorecard should contain the always-on operational metrics, one speed metric chosen from the use-case kind, one to five quality metrics, and zero to five risk gates. Each row has runtime_status. The default selector proposes only metrics score() implements, judge() rows (one rubric call, pairwise in both orders), and the timing, token, and cost metrics the runner records. Planned rows stay in the bank. Set include_planned to opt in. If filtering would leave quality empty, the selector uses an implemented metric for that kind, or rubric_score. Document vision is included for pages and scans: character and word error, plus box overlap. Speech, image aesthetics, and caption preference are not. The bank is a library, not a list to run in full.

159 metrics sit in 22 categories (5 avoid_as_primary, 53 core, 24 diagnostic, 77 supporting). Core rows are the ones a selector should consider first when the brief matches. The avoid-as-primary rows exist so BLEU, ROUGE-L, BERTScore, summary fluency, and embedding cosine can be refused as the quality score. The remaining rows are supporting measurements or diagnostics.

A row earned a place when three things were true. A client can compute it on their own cases. Two teams would otherwise implement it under different names. And the number can change a ship decision: quality, a hard gate, speed, cost, or a business outcome. A public exam fails the first test. An alias fails the second. A vanity score fails the third.

The existing product catalogue, in docs/metrics-catalog.md, already fixed forty ids. Every one of those ids is in this bank with the same spelling, so a later biy_metrics_list can serve this file without a rename. This folder does not edit that catalogue. The bank is the superset.

A client scorecard is not the bank. It is a few operational metrics, one speed metric chosen from the kind, one to five quality metrics, and zero to five risk gates. The reference selector in src/metric_bank/selector.py applies that budget, and by default it keeps only rows the runtime can compute. Planned rows stay in the bank. A brief sets include_planned to opt in. The five clients further down are the default cards.

What was studied, and what was left out

HELM is the argument for the shape of the bank. Liang and colleagues scored accuracy together with calibration, robustness, fairness, toxicity, and efficiency, and they warned against a win rate that depends on who else was in the set. That is why quality here is an absolute 0–1 mean, not a rank against the other candidates, and why speed and cost stay on their own axes.

MMLU, MMLU-Pro, BIG-bench, BBH, GSM8K, MATH, and GPQA measure stored knowledge or textbook reasoning. They are useful as a prior that a model family has a skill. They are useless as the score for a claims extractor or a support bot, because the client's wrong answers are different. The scoring idea that does transfer — final-answer equivalence for math, atomic-fact precision for long biographies, attempted-versus-correct for short factoid questions — is in the bank as math_equivalence, factscore, and short_form_factuality.

Ragas, DeepEval, and the TruLens RAG triad name the same grounding constructs more than once. Faithfulness, answer relevancy, context precision, and context recall each have one row. The other library's name is an alias. Summary consistency is an alias of faithfulness, not a second quality term. Where a claim-level ratio and an NLI classifier estimate the same thing, the claim-level ratio is primary and the classifier (groundedness_nli) is the cheaper alternate.

IFEval contributes the instruction metrics: a prompt passes strictly when every verifiable constraint passes on the raw output. The client's constraints replace the public constraint list, which frontier models have saturated. BFCL contributes tool-call AST match, tool choice, and irrelevance. τ-bench contributes task success in a sandbox and pass^k, the probability that all k trials succeed. SWE-bench contributes patch resolution when the client has a repository and tests. Those are rows. The leaderboards themselves are priors.

OpenAI Evals contributes the small deterministic assertions: exact match, contains, and a pattern. LMSYS Arena and AlpacaEval contribute pairwise comparison against a fixed baseline, both orders, and a warning that uncorrected win rates follow length. The length-controlled win rate is in the bank as a diagnostic, not as the quality score. Artificial Analysis contributes the operational vocabulary — time to first token, output tokens per second, price — and is explicit that an intelligence index is not the client's quality.

Safety and bias are gates and diagnostics, not quality-average terms. BBQ's ambiguous-context score is signed and best at zero, so it cannot enter a higher-better average. RealToxicityPrompts, XSTest, and HarmBench justify measuring toxicity, over-refusal, and harmful compliance on a reviewed set. The bank records how to score those sets. It does not contain attack strings, and the selector is told not to synthesize any in order to produce a number.

Perplexity is not comparable across tokenizers and is not a task score, so it is not a row. MAUVE compares a distribution of generations to a reference distribution; a client with a few dozen cases cannot estimate it, and it does not say whether the answer was supported. Speech, image aesthetics, and caption preference are out of scope. Document vision is in scope: when the model reads a page, character error, word error, and box overlap are things the client can compute from a transcription and a set of rectangles. Arena Elo was left out because it moves when the field of opponents moves.

How a row is shaped

Every metric carries an id, a name, a category, a description, what it measures, when to use it, when not to, the inputs it requires, a computation method and formula, a score range with an interpretation, a cost, known limitations, related metrics, and tags. Around that, the selector needs a few controls the prose fields cannot express.

  • utility_axis is quality, speed, cost, gate_only, or none. Only quality enters Q, and only when the stored score is higher-better on 0–1.
  • quality_transform is identity for those quality rows and not_a_quality_input otherwise. Nothing in this bank asks the runner to subtract a risk rate from 1 and then average it.
  • group_role separates a primary measurement from its alternate, its gate form, a diagnostic, and a judging method. Hallucination rate is the gate form of grounding. Faithfulness is the primary.
  • selector_priority is core, supporting, diagnostic, or avoid_as_primary.
  • runtime_status is implemented, judge, runner_measured, or planned. The default selector proposes the first three. score() computes implemented ids only and raises on the others.
  • Inputs are a closed vocabulary: references, labels, retrieved context, human raters, an LLM judge, logs, prices, a schema, a tool trace, and the rest of the list in the schema. If the client cannot supply the input, the metric is not eligible.

The schema is schema/metric-bank.schema.json (JSON Schema draft 2020-12, additional properties forbidden). validate.py checks the data file against that schema, against build_bank(), and against the catalogue ids.

Five scorecards

The default card keeps the operational metrics computed from result rows: error rate, truncation, throttle, cost per case, cost per success, and output tokens per case, plus time to first token for streamed text or end-to-end latency for a finished object. Cost per success is the cost used for ranking. Classification uses classification_f1, which the ranking computes over a candidate's cases. If a kind has no runnable quality metric, the quality slot is rubric_score. An agent with no tool trace says that fallback in the card notes. An agent with tools uses tool_call_correct and tool_choice_correct.

Selection rules, the signal table, and a prompt for the app's model are in selection/GUIDE.md. The cards below are the reference selector, not a hand-edited illustration, so they stay aligned with the guide and the tests.

How this lines up with BIY scoring

The product ranks in three steps: gates, then the Pareto front, then utility. Gates disqualify. A schema failure, an error rate above the kind's ceiling, a confirmed privacy leak, or a latency ceiling the client named are gates. A preference is not.

Quality Q is the weighted mean of the scorecard's quality metrics. Each one is already on 0–1, higher better. Q is not rescaled against the other candidates in the run. A model that scores 0.9 is a 0.9 even if every candidate is excellent.

Speed S and cost C use the log target from the architecture. At or under the target the score is 1. At or over the maximum it is 0. Between them it is 1 minus log(x / target) divided by log(max / target). Speed uses a metric the runner records: time to first token for chat, end-to-end for a sync call, and nothing for a batch. Agent task wall time is planned, so the default agent card ranks on end-to-end latency. Cost uses dollars per successful case. Utility is the weighted geometric mean of Q, S, and C. A zero on a weighted axis sinks the candidate.

The judge, when one is used, is pinned, from a different family than the candidates, and pairwise runs are judged in both orders. The bank's judging category is an instrument. Rubric score, G-Eval, and pairwise against a baseline compete for a single slot. They do not stack on top of a named metric for the same construct.

Illustrative formulas — token F1, classification F1, faithfulness from claim verdicts, the cost identity, expected calibration error, unbiased pass@k and pass^k, ROUGE-L, and the log target — live in src/metric_bank/illustrative.py and are exercised in the notebook. They do not call a model.
